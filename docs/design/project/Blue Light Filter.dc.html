Five icons, one arc.

Same constraint as the app: no illustration, no gradient as decoration, no glow, no pure white. Each option is drawn from geometry already in the product, the arc, the ramp, the knob, so the launcher icon is recognisably the same object as the Home screen readout. All shown at 108px, then at 40px and 24px, because a launcher icon that only works large is a logo, not an icon.

Notes
My pick: A. It is the only option that is already in the app, the knob on the arc the user drags every time. The icon and the interface teach each other, and nothing else in the launcher looks like it.
Runner-up: B if you want the strongest silhouette at 24px and are willing to live near sunset iconography.
Adaptive icon. Foreground is the arc alone, background the flat near-black surface, so Android's mask crops safely at any shape. Nothing critical enters the outer 18% of the canvas.
Monochrome and themed-icon variant. All five reduce to a single-colour stroke without losing meaning, since none depends on the colour to be read, only on the arc's fill fraction.
The icon does not track state. No badge, no fill reflecting the current warmth. A launcher icon that changes is an attention mechanic, and this product does not have those.
Notification small icon is the same arc at 24px, stroke only, no knob, which is option A's 24px rendering exactly.

Gesture model · annotated
Anywhere is the control. Pointer-down anywhere below the status bar arms the gesture. There is no track to find in the dark and nothing to aim at, the value moves relative to where you started, so touching never causes a jump.
Vertical = warmth. Full screen height spans 4600K → 1600K, about 4.5K per pixel. Deliberately coarse: a 40px thumb twitch moves ~180K, below the noticeable-difference threshold in a dark room.
Horizontal = dimming. Full width spans 100% → 8%. Rightward always brightens, so the recovery direction is the same as the reading direction of the arc.
Axis dominance. Diagonal drags apply both, but the dominant axis is weighted 1.4× so a sloppy vertical drag doesn't dim by accident. No axis lock, locking feels broken when your eyes are closed.
The arc is the readout. The knob rides the same arc the Schedule screen draws; warmth is its position along the day. No progress bar, no track, no ticks.
Detents. Soft snap within ±60K of any preset value, one haptic tick each, pullable and never sticky. Ticks are the only feedback available with the screen at 8%.
Numerals fade back. Full opacity while touching; 500ms after release they crossfade to 22% over 600ms. They never move, so the fade reads as light dropping, not as an element leaving.
TalkBack. The surface exposes two seekbars, Warmth and Brightness, with 10% increments, spoken values, and volume-key adjustment. The gesture is an enhancement, never the only path.
Dark-room readability. Body text holds ≥4.6:1 against its surface at every point on the ramp, but the largest numerals sit at 3.2:1 on purpose, they are 96px, and a higher ratio here is glare, not legibility.

Schedule notes
Orientation. The arc always runs 18:00 → 09:00 left to right, night at the base of both ends. Evening is on the left because that is where the thumb starts a drag.
Anchor targets. The visible ring is 20px, the touch target 56px, with a concentric hairline showing the grab radius so it can be found without a hit.
Offsets by drag. Dragging past sunset reads "30 min before sunset"; the label rewrites live in 5-minute steps with a tick at exact sunset. No number entry anywhere.
Ramp. A scheduled start crossfades over the set duration, the arc's fill grows at the same rate the screen warms. No toast, no notification sound, nothing that announces itself.
TalkBack. Each anchor is a seekbar in 5-minute increments announcing "Start, 30 minutes before sunset, 8:41 PM".

Why this reads as trustworthy
It names the alarming thing first, in Android's own words, before asking for anything. Nothing hides behind "learn more".
Every claim is falsifiable, a permission list the user can verify in Settings, not a promise about intentions.
"Not now" is a real exit at full weight, never greyed. A dead end here is what makes a permission feel coerced.
No illustration, no shield, no green checkmarks, no lock icons. Reassurance graphics are what malware uses; a plain list is what an honest utility uses.
This screen appears cool-grey because the filter is not running yet. It is the only screen in the product the user sees before the ramp exists, and the first tint after granting is the reward.

Tokens are not fixed values, they are functions of two inputs: warmth and dim. Every surface interpolates in Oklch along this row, so hue rotates and chroma rises together while lightness stays pinned to its step, a 2400K surface is never brighter than the same token at 3400K. Dim multiplies lightness afterwards, uniformly, which is why contrast ratios hold at every brightness.

Elevation on true black. No shadows, a shadow needs light behind the surface to exist. A raised surface is +1 step on the ramp (about +3% lightness) plus, only where a boundary must be unambiguous, a 1px hairline at 8–14% of the surface's own hue. Three levels, no more: ground, sheet, control.
MOTION · BEHAVES LIKE LIGHT
Colour crossfade · 420ms
cubic-bezier(.32,.02,.2,1). Every tint change interpolates; nothing steps. Values under the thumb update in real time, surfaces trail slightly behind, that lag is what makes it feel like light rather than a repaint.
Value fade-back · 600ms
Numerals hold 500ms after release, then fall to 22% opacity. No motion, only opacity.
Scheduled ramp · the full duration
A 45-minute ramp is a 45-minute crossfade. It is never announced; the only trace is the notification's subtitle rewriting itself.
No springs. No overshoot, no bounce, no scale-in. Sheets rise 520ms with a soft-out curve and fade their scrim rather than sliding fast.
Haptics carry the precision. One light tick per preset detent, one heavier tick at range ends. The screen may be unreadable; the ticks are not.
Reduced motion. Crossfades shorten to 120ms but are never removed, an instant tint change is a flash, which is the one thing this product must never do.
DARK-ROOM & ACCESSIBILITY
No pure white, ever, text/hi tops out at #E9ECEE, and only the panic strip is allowed a light background.
Nothing appears without being asked for. No modals, no toasts, no snackbars that were not caused by a touch.
Bottom third holds every primary control, power, presets, and the sheet actions all sit within 260px of the bottom edge.
Targets 44px minimum, 56px for anything used in the dark, and the arc anchors get 56px invisible padding.
TalkBack reads state before name ("On, filter, 1700 kelvin"), and every colour-carrying element also carries a monogram or a spec.
